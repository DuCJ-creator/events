Passage 1: The Core That Lies in ChungHsin Culture

Culture—*wen hua* in Chinese—begins with what humans leave behind. *Wen* refers to the humanities:the marks we humans leave on Earthwith our own hands. *Hua* refers to internalization:the accumulated experiences of a group of peoplein their daily lives,gradually absorbed into a shared way of living. Simply put,culture is the shared lifestyle and essencethat a group of people preserves and passes down. ChungHsin culture, then, is the shared wayof learning, living, and beingthat defines all of us at ChungHsin.

This way of life has clear, visible expressions. We live itthrough every aspect of our daily routine:our dress code,our Qunyu Ban (a class-based collectivity program),our dining etiquette using chopsticks,our morning assemblies, and more. Its true essence, however, remains invisible. It is intangible yet real,and it shapes our lives:the values we hold,our optimistic attitude,and our relentless spirit of striving.

ChungHsin is a schooldedicated to carrying forward and promotingthe finest traditions of Chinese culture. We welcome students and parentswho affirm the essence of a ChungHsin education,who are willing to make commitments,and who expect to receive a quality education—and only those who do. On this foundation,a unique ChungHsin learning culture has taken shape.

ChungHsin's learning emphasizes honesty. *Chung* means being honest with yourself,and *Hsin* means being honest with others. Only by practicing both *Chung* and *Hsin*can we live together in harmony.

Passage 2: Living Our Principles and Honoring Our Heritage

At ChungHsin,education centers on three timeless virtues:Zhi (Wisdom), Ren (Benevolence), and Yong (Courage). From a Confucian view,good character means knowing the good—Zhi, the cognitive dimension;loving the good—Ren, the dimension of feeling and emotion;and doing the good—Yong, the dimension of action and behavior. We try to incorporate this knowing, loving, and doing of the goodinto all aspects of our educational approach,since character building—the engraving of good qualities—is a fundamental element of education.

We also practice Shu—reciprocity, or mutual respect—putting ourselves in others' shoes:"Do not do to otherswhat you would not wish done to yourself." We practice self-discipline as well,knowing that only through self-disciplinecan we achieve true freedom.

When we distill these qualitiesand place them into the daily learning life of everyone at ChungHsin,they become our school motto:"Be Optimistic and Strive;Sincerely Hold Fast to the Mean." They become our school ethos:“Perfect the self through Chung,serve others with Hsin;walk with others in the Mean,rooted in Shu;seek truth and substance,persevere with constancy;rejoice in our community,and let our voice ring out.”

Every year,ChungHsin Culture Month is held around the anniversary of Dr. Sun Yat-sen's birthday. It is a time to honor our roots, our school, and our heritage. At the same time,it challenges us to study diligently,to strive upward,to think independently,and to rise above distractions—not to fear those who play tricks and put on airs,not to become blind followers of others,but to stand tall, proud and upright,with both Chung and Hsin.

Passage 3: The Purpose of Education

“What is the purpose of education?” The theme of this year's PAPE (the Pan-Pacific Association of Private School Education) Congress,“The Total Development of the Human Person,”provides us with an opportunityto further re-examine this extensively debated question.

Education has been seen as a toolto sustain social progress and national development;in fact,they were the major functions of public schoolingin the past 200 years. In the last two to three decades,there has been a strong voiceadvocating the development of the whole person in educationby pointing outthat education should not only be primarily involved in shaping and molding our young peopleinto future workers or citizens. But, education should also be concernedwith all aspects of a person.

I believethat my colleagues from Taiwan and educators here,accept the ideathat the development of the whole personis our fundamental responsibility as educators. More importantly,such an idea is exactlywhat we as private school educators have been insisting upon and striving for over the yearsin order to help our young peopleto achieve their potential.

Passage 4: Holistic Education

By “holistic”I mean we are concerned with the whole human beingas a fully functioning integrative system—the entire human essence---rather than just dealing with an isolated elementlike only the academic part.

We are dedicated to providing a positive learning environmentthat emphasizes the development of the whole personfor meeting the challenges of living. As well as,we are committed to helping each personto reach his/her potentialfor achieving human goodness and even greatness.

Holistic education is a processthat fully engages the mind, body, emotions, and spirit…all of which are nurtured simultaneously. In our approach,we blend together six interrelated realms of inquiry and developmentas part of our concept of providing “comprehensive education”in order to educate the whole person.

This six-dimensional model includes:Cognitive-Academic Development;Emotional Intelligence (EQ);Character-Building;Citizenship;Spirituality;and, Fitness and Health.

Passage 5: Empowering Future Global Citizens

As educators,we face enormous challenges in the 21st century,the paramount one is the need to help our studentsto become aware and prepared to live in a worldvastly different from the one most of us were raised in. We can no longer afford to only teach our studentswhat has been taught in the past. Instead,we need to seek out new paradigms,and one such framework is the holistic educational approachdiscussed here today.

We now live in a “global village”,which is borderless,increasing in complexity,and changing at an accelerating pace. Given this context,we as educators need to provide our studentswith a holistic “world view” of life on the Earth,and the emerging global community. Learning holistic perspectives will enable our studentsto perceive and understand the various contextsthat shape and give meaning to life.

Furthermore,a holistic approach offers educators an opportunityto facilitate the development of whole human beings,who are capable of dealing with life as a whole. A significant aspect of our holistic educational approach isthat we can inculcate in our students a real passionate love for learningthat will last for their entire lifetimes.

Finally,we can lead our students along the pathtoward accepting being responsible to themselves,to their societies and countries,and to becoming participating world citizens.
